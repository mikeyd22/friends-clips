# On-Screen Hook Title Guide

Supplied by Michael for Method 2. Install to `System/` alongside the method
spec when approved.

## Terminology

The clearest name is **on-screen hook text** / **hook title**. Distinct from:

- **Subtitles** — transcription of spoken words.
- **Post caption** — the description below the uploaded video.
- **Clip title/name** — an internal name that may later inspire the hook text.

## 1. What is a hook title?

Short text shown near the beginning of a video to stop the viewer scrolling.
It is **not** a word-for-word subtitle, the post description, a summary of
everything that happens, or a substitute for an interesting clip.

A good hook title gives the viewer:

1. Enough context to understand the situation
2. A specific reason to continue watching
3. An unanswered question the clip will resolve

## 2. Is a hook title needed?

Optional. **Use one when:** the clip begins mid-conversation; the subject or
situation is initially unclear; the opening sentence takes too long to
establish stakes; the first few seconds are visually quiet; the viewer needs
context from outside the clip; the title makes the payoff easier to anticipate
without spoiling it.

**Skip it when:** the opening line already creates strong curiosity; the action
is immediately understandable; the title would just repeat the speaker; the
text would cover an important visual; the title reveals the answer too early;
you cannot write an interesting title without exaggerating.

## 3. The four jobs

- **Stop** — make subject, stakes, surprise, or relevance immediately apparent.
- **Orient** — give cold viewers the missing context.
- **Open a loop** — create a specific unanswered question.
- **Make a truthful promise** — the clip must deliver the answer, reveal,
  consequence, or emotional payoff the title suggests.

## 4. Reliable structures

Context + unusual event · Expectation vs reality · Specific unanswered question ·
Stakes or consequence · Surprising claim · Emotional turn · Audience relevance ·
Quoted reaction · Payoff preview.

Use quotation marks only when the person actually says it, or for a faithful
shortened quotation. Avoid unsupported universals ("everyone", "nobody",
"the best") unless the clip genuinely supports them.

## 5. Writing process

1. **Identify the real payoff** — "By the end, the viewer discovers ______."
2. **Identify the missing context** — what would confuse a first-time viewer?
3. **Find the strongest honest gap** — what question stays open at the start,
   and gets resolved inside the clip?
4. **Write at least ten options** across different angles.
5. **Remove unnecessary words**, then make it more specific if the footage allows.
6. **Check the promise** — does the clip genuinely deliver what the title implies?

## 6. Writing rules

Understandable on first read · specific to the clip · short enough to read
quickly · ordinary spoken language · interesting without deception · accurate
about who did or said what · connected to a payoff inside the clip.

~3–8 words is a useful starting point; clarity matters more than word count.
Prefer concrete nouns and verbs over vague intensifiers.

## 7. Avoid

Revealing the entire ending · inventing conflict or outrage · removing an
important qualifier · turning opinion into fact · misattributing words ·
unrelated celebrities or topics · relying on "shocking/crazy/insane" ·
promising a reveal that happens outside the clip · repeating the spoken opening
without adding clarity · needing several lines of explanation.

Be especially careful with medical, financial, legal, political, safety, and
reputational claims.

## 8. Visual presentation

Inside the platform-safe area · away from faces and important action · one or
two lines · large, highly readable type · strong contrast · backing, shadow, or
outline over busy backgrounds · shown immediately when needed for orientation ·
visible long enough to read once comfortably · removed when it competes with
the scene · reviewed muted at phone size.

Never place essential text near the bottom description strip or beside the
platform interaction buttons.

### Measured typography settings (from the clip-2 comparison)

Tested over real footage at 1080x1920. See `hook-variants.png` and
`hook-refined.png`.

| Setting | Value |
|---|---|
| Target | **2 lines at 95–105px** |
| Fallback | 3 lines at ~90px, only when context cannot be cut |
| Never | 4+ lines — reads as a wall and strands orphan words |
| Font | TikTok Sans Variable, weight 900 |
| Fill / stroke | white, 6px black stroke |
| Max text width | 640px, centred (right edge 860, clear of the rail at 875) |
| Vertical position | centred ~y470, upper third |
| Emoji | inline at end of last line with a clear space, never floating below |
| Line balance | never leave 1–2 short words alone on the last line |

**Consequence for writing:** type size is what stops the scroll, and length
costs type size. Aim for **4–7 words**. A hook needing more than that is usually
doing too much work.

## 9. Checklist

Answer yes to all before approving: can a new viewer understand it immediately ·
does it identify a subject, situation, or stake · does it create a specific
unanswered question · does the clip answer it · is every factual implication
accurate · does it preserve the speaker's meaning · is it more useful than the
opening footage alone · readable on a phone · avoids covering essential visuals ·
would the clip still be worth watching without deceptive wording.

If the promise or meaning check fails, do not use the title.

## 10. Testing

For important clips prepare version A (no hook title), B (clear contextual
title), C (curiosity-focused title). Compare early retention, average watch
time, completion, rewatches, shares, comments. Views alone cannot prove the
title caused the result. Record which structures work for this account.

## House style

From Michael's own TikToks — selective CAPS on the emphasised words:

- `[person 1] REALIZED how SMART [person 2] is after he [action] 👀`
- `[person] FULLY CRASHES OUT AFTER MAKING 0 PROGRESS in 48 HOURS`
- Sometimes the literal first spoken line is the hook.
